The permit queue is now the climate plan

The country has approved enough wind and solar to meet its 2035 target twice over. Almost none of it has a date for a grid connection.

JW
Jonas Whitlock in the capital
28 October 2026 · 5 min read

There is a spreadsheet, maintained by a small team at the energy regulator, that is in effect the national climate plan. It has 4,112 rows. Each is a project that wants to connect to the grid, with a capacity, a location and a date.

Add up the capacity column and the country has already met its target for 2035, and then met it again. Read the date column and the picture changes. The median project in the list has been offered a connection in 2034.

"We do not have a shortage of ambition," says a senior official who has worked on the list for six years. "We have a queue."

First come, first served

The queue exists because connections were, until this year, handed out in the order people asked. Asking was cheap. A developer with an option on a field could reserve a place for the price of a form, and many reserved several, intending to build the one that worked out.

The result is a list in which, by the regulator's estimate, six projects in ten will never be built, and the four that will are stuck behind them.

Reform began in March. Projects must now show planning consent and financing to keep their place, and those that cannot are moved to the back. In the first six months 1,300 rows were removed.

That helps, but it does not build anything. The scarce thing was never a place in the list. It is transformers, cable and the engineers who install them, and for those the lead time is still measured in years.

Who jumps ahead

Some places have stopped waiting for their turn. A valley co-operative that bought the substation it depends on now connects local projects in weeks, because it owns the piece of the network that was the bottleneck.

Officials are in two minds about this. It works, and it is not obviously fair to the developer in the next valley who followed the rules and is still in row 2,907.

The regulator's answer, due in the new year, is expected to let communities buy into local network upgrades in exchange for earlier dates.

None of which appears in the climate plan the government published, a document of 212 pages with a wind turbine on the cover. The spreadsheet has no cover.

It was last updated on Tuesday. The median date moved forward by eleven days.
